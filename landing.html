A research assistant that runs on your own machine.

ThinkStack reads the papers you give it, shows you where the literature is thin, and drafts in LaTeX. The models, the index and your documents stay on the device. There is no account and no server.

What it does

Reads your library

Drop in PDFs. Title, authors and year are read from the geometry of the page rather than guessed from flattened text, and every passage is embedded locally so the collection can be searched by meaning instead of by keyword.

Finds what is missing

Papers are placed by similarity and grouped into themes. Claims that nothing in the collection supports, and questions nothing answers, are marked on the map where the literature is thin.

Drafts in LaTeX

Describe a section in plain language and get compilable markup, at the cursor. Cite a paper from your library and the bibliography writes itself; the document compiles on your machine, offline.

How it works
01

Ingest

A PDF becomes text, then passages, then vectors. Bibliographic detail is taken from the layout of the first page. All of it happens on the machine in front of you.

02

Map

Passages cluster into themes and papers are laid out by semantic distance. The interesting result is not the clusters but the space between them.

03

Write

The editor turns instructions into LaTeX, grounded in the papers you select, and compiles to PDF with a typesetting engine shipped inside the application.

Built on
Inference Qwen2.5 at 0.5B and 1.5B parameters, run locally through llama.cpp, routed per task rather than one model for everything.
Retrieval Sentence-transformer embeddings in an on-disk vector store. No index leaves the machine.
Extraction Title and authors read from page geometry. 92.9% of titles and 85.7% of author lists exactly correct across 56 papers sampled at random from 15 arXiv categories, scored against the archive's own records.
Typesetting A Tectonic engine bundled with a warmed package cache, so the first compile needs no network.
Application A Rust and Tauri 2 shell around a React 19 interface and a FastAPI backend, distributed as one installer per platform.
Verification 1,276 automated tests across four layers, run on Linux, macOS and Windows before any release is published.
